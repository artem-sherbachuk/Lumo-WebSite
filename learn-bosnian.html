Bosnian and its sister languages

Bosnian is one of the four BCMS standards — Bosnian, Croatian, Montenegrin, Serbian — that grew from one South Slavic root and remain completely mutually intelligible. It uses the soft ijekavian vowels it shares with Croatian (lijepo, mlijeko), is written mainly in Latin script, and adds its own unmistakable warmth: centuries of Ottoman history left Bosnian with beautiful words like merhaba (hello, among Bosniaks), ćejf (the art of savoring a moment), and sevdah (a sweet melancholy with its own music genre).

For a learner the math is wonderful: learn the shared core once, and Sarajevo, Zagreb, Belgrade, and Kotor all open at the same time — around 16 million speakers.

How Lumo gets you speaking Bosnian

  • The right variant, built in. Lumo shows Croatian and Serbian side by side — and the ijekavian forms it teaches (mlijeko, gdje, lijepo) are precisely the ones Bosnian uses.
  • Speaking from minute one. Every phrase is said out loud with instant word-by-word feedback. No silent studying.
  • A story that carries you. 30 chapters from A1 to C2 — cafés, markets, buses, beaches — the same situations you'll live in Bosnia's kafanas and čaršijas.
  • Cyrillic included. Bosnia uses Latin script day to day, but Cyrillic appears too — and Lumo teaches you to read both without fear.

Your first Bosnian phrases

Dobar dan!Good day! — always right, everywhere. Molim jednu kafu.One coffee, please — in the land of legendary Bosnian coffee, the most important sentence of all. Hvala lijepo!Thank you kindly — note the ijekavian “lijepo,” pure Bosnian music. Gdje je stara čaršija?Where is the old bazaar?
