Wait — is Montenegrin its own language?

Montenegrin is the official language of Montenegro and a proud member of the BCMS family — Bosnian, Croatian, Montenegrin, Serbian — four standards so close that speakers understand each other completely. In practice, the language you hear in Kotor's old town or a Podgorica kafana is built from the same core as Croatian and Serbian, with Montenegro's own melody: the soft ijekavian vowels (lijepo, mlijeko) it shares with the coast, and both Latin and Cyrillic scripts on its street signs.

This is the honest secret of the region: learn the shared core once, and all four doors open. That's exactly how Lumo teaches.

Why Montenegrin is the perfect target for Lumo's method

  • The story lives where the language lives. Lumo's 30-chapter journey runs along the Adriatic — a harbor café, a morning market, the bus to Budva, a beach at sunset. You learn the words in the places you'll actually say them.
  • Ijekavian and ekavian, side by side. Lumo shows Croatian and Serbian variants together (mlijeko / mleko), so Montenegro's ijekavian speech feels native to you from the start.
  • Both scripts, no fear. Montenegro uses Latin and Cyrillic interchangeably — and so does Lumo, on every phrase.
  • Speaking first. Say every phrase out loud, get instant word-by-word feedback. The neighbor's “Dobar dan!” deserves a confident reply.

Living in Montenegro? This is your unfair advantage

Thousands of expats and long-stay travelers call Budva, Kotor, Tivat, and Podgorica home — and most never get past hvala. The moment you do, Montenegro changes: the market seller rounds prices down instead of up, the landlord becomes a friend, the kafana table finds room for one more chair. Montenegrins don't expect foreigners to learn their language, which is precisely why trying is so powerful.

In Montenegro, ten phrases spoken with a smile are worth more than fluent English.
